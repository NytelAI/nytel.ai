Real trials, real numbers

Planning a trial means asking: how many patients, or how many events? These plots use the same math as the lab.

A worked design

The drug improves a score by 3 points (the effect size). Patients vary by about 10 points (the SD).

Chance to catch it (power):

Clinical Trials in Practice gets about 467 patients for this design. Patients needed grow with (noise divided by effect) squared, so half the improvement needs four times the patients.

Cancer trials count events

An event is a patient whose cancer grew or who died. The trial waits for enough of them. Hazard ratio (HR) 0.5 means half the hazard, the risk at each moment.

HR 0.5 needs about 88 events. FOCUS4-N, a UK colorectal cancer trial, planned 635 events for HR 0.8.

Rare side effects need crowds

ICH E1 asks for about 1,500 patients exposed to a drug taken long-term for a non-life-threatening condition. Pick a true side-effect rate.

Why sample size is a money question

Average cost per study across therapeutic areas, as listed in Clinical Trials in Practice, citing Sertkaya et al. (2016) and Moore et al. (2018). Phase II is a few hundred patients to find a dose; Phase III is large trials to prove the drug works. More patients means a bigger bill, and each grid step multiplies the cost by about 2 to 2.5.

Sources: design numbers from Sokolova and Sokolov (2026), Clinical Trials in Practice. FOCUS4-N: Adams R.A. et al. (2021), J Clin Oncol 39:3693-3704, J Clin Oncol 2021 paper (UCL Discovery): 644 patients and 635 events for 80% power at HR 0.8, two-sided 5%. Safety numbers: ICH E1 guideline.

Regulator's corner

The ICH E20 draft describes a false alarm as "conclusions that truly inefficacious treatments are efficacious" (useless drugs called useful). That is our α, the Type I error.

E20 covers adaptive designs, which change course mid-trial by rules written in advance.

ICH E20, section 3.3 (draft, June 2025, not final). Read the guideline (FDA).

Takeaway

  • A trial can cry wolf (Type I, α) or sleep through a real wolf (Type II, β). Power (the chance to catch a real drug) is 1 minus β.
  • Only more patients shrink both errors at once. Moving the bar just trades one for the other.
  • Half the effect costs about four times the patients.
