A real case: borrowing from an earlier trial

FDA's Bayesian draft names the REBYOTA program, a treatment for C. difficile, a gut infection. An earlier phase 2 trial helped the later analysis; FDA calls this dynamic borrowing: borrow less when the old data disagrees with the new.

Earlier trial (toy: 20 patients). Teal = counted.
New trial (toy: 30 patients).

Toy numbers, not REBYOTA data. FDA says good methods "borrow less when the data are less similar and more when the data are more similar" (Section V). Source: FDA draft guidance Use of Bayesian Methodology in Clinical Trials, Sections III.A and V; Sokolova, Sokolov and Polson, arXiv:2602.09208.

Regulator's corner

FDA says a Type A meeting is for a program that is stuck:

"necessary for an otherwise stalled drug or biological product development program to proceed or to address an important safety issue."

Section III. FDA, Formal Meetings Between the FDA and Sponsors or Applicants of PDUFA Products (August 2026).

Takeaway

  • The road has stages, and FDA has a touch point at each big step.
  • Pick the meeting that fits the question: stuck, early, or small.
  • The sponsor answers for the trial, even when a CRO does the work.
