Module 12

Decisions: what Type I control costs

Every rule for saying "yes" trades false alarms against missed drugs. What does safety cost in patients?

Jump to an example:
Words used here, in plain terms
  • Effect size: how big the benefit is, such as 3 points of improvement. Standard error (SE): how far a result typically lands from the truth just by luck, so "1.5 SE" is a result 1.5 of those steps from zero (module 2).
  • Type I error: approving a drug that does nothing. Type II error: missing a drug that works. Power: the chance a trial finds a real effect (module 3).
  • Alpha: the false-alarm chance we allow. One-sided counts only "the drug is better"; two-sided also counts "worse". Posterior: what we believe about the drug after seeing the data (module 1).

C. What FDA asks for

The January 2026 draft guidance on Bayesian methods (Section IV.A) lists three routes to a success criterion. Only the first is tied to Type I error. Read it at fda.gov.

Route 1

Calibrate to Type I error

"... can be chosen such that the overall FWER is controlled at a given level, typically 0.025 one-sided"

Default bar: familywise error "no greater than 0.025, one-sided". FWER is the chance of any false alarm among all the tests (module 4).

Route 2

Read the posterior directly

Use a prior that accurately sums up what is already known, then judge the posterior itself.

The rule reads Pr(d > a) > c. Win if the chance that the drug beats the margin a is above c.

Route 3

Weigh benefit against risk

Decision-theoretic criteria, like the cost ratio in section A.

FDA's own heading (IV.A.3): "Success Criteria Based on Benefit-Risk Assessment or Decision-Theoretic Approaches". Benefit-risk means: do the gains outweigh the harms?

Takeaway

  • Every rule for saying yes trades false alarms against missed drugs and patients.
  • FDA's default bar is a 0.025 false-alarm chance, one-sided.
  • A Bayesian rule usually meets it by tuning the rule with pretend trials. Designs that borrow outside data may agree another criterion with FDA.
